# Week 5 Notes

This folder contains my Week 5 research materials on reliable AI safety evaluations.

## Week focus

This week shifts from building evaluation pipelines to thinking more critically about what makes an evaluation trustworthy enough for high-stakes settings such as safety, deployment, and policy.

The core themes are:

- why safety benchmarks cannot be treated like ordinary capability benchmarks
- why mature evaluation requires clearer measurement objects, coverage, reproducibility, and stronger statistical practice
- why research taste matters, especially when easy-to-measure questions crowd out important but harder safety questions

## Goal statement

My goal in this course is to build the judgment and practical skill to design evaluations that are not only technically correct, but genuinely useful for AI safety decisions. I want to grow toward work where I can connect evaluation design, research rigor, and real-world deployment questions, especially in settings where weak benchmarks or shallow metrics could create false confidence about safety.

## Main takeaway

The main lesson of Week 5 is that reliable AI safety evaluation is not just about running benchmarks well. It is about choosing the right objects to measure, understanding the limits of those measurements, and staying focused on important safety questions even when they are harder to operationalize.
